04 Medical & sensitive data

Prepare useful data. Control sensitive information.

De-identification workflows for structured records and free text, designed around the intended use and access model. Combine identifier detection, masking or pseudonymization, evaluation and review of residual disclosure risk.

Define what to protect

Identify direct identifiers and contextual information that could expose an individual. Establish rules for the data types, language and downstream task.

Evaluate the transformation

Measure missed identifiers and unnecessary removal on representative examples. Assess how the changes affect the usefulness of the data.

Connect to approved AI workflows

Define what information may reach an LLM or multimodal service, where it may be processed and how access is controlled. De-identification alone is not a guarantee of anonymity.

05 How an engagement works

From clinical question to evidence.

Four steps, each with a clear output. You can stop after any of them.

01

Feasibility & data audit

We review the clinical question, the endpoint and the data you have. You get a short go/no-go with the risks we see, before any model is built.

02

Retrospective validation

We build on historical data and test on patients, sites or time periods the model never saw. Results come with confidence intervals, subgroup checks and known failure modes.

03

Prospective evaluation plan

We draft how the model will be tested going forward, for example in silent (shadow) mode. The plan sets endpoints and sample size, and your team can take it to the ethics committee (IRB).

04

Integration & monitoring

We plan how the model reaches the workflow, through the EMR, FHIR or your device software. We also define who reviews its output and how drift is caught after go-live.

06 Questions buyers ask

Straight answers.

Can you work with data that can’t leave the hospital?

Yes. We can work inside your research environment under your access controls, or on de-identified extracts prepared for the task. Hosting, access and contracts are agreed with the data owner before work starts.

Do you help with FDA or CE evidence?

We produce the evaluation and validation evidence that regulatory work relies on: the study design, performance with uncertainty, subgroup results and documented limitations. We are not regulatory consultants, and engaging MLAIA does not imply any regulatory status for your product.

How do you avoid leakage and bias?

We split data by patient and by site, never by record or image, and lock the test set early. We report performance per subgroup and per site, so a gap shows up before a reviewer finds it.

Our dataset is small, or comes from several sites. Is that a problem?

Not always. We check data quality, cohort make-up and what evaluation the data can support. If the data limits what can be concluded, we say so up front.

What do we get at the end?

Depending on the step: a feasibility memo, an evaluation plan, a validation report, the code and model, and an integration path. Everything is documented so your team can own it.

What do you need from us to start?

The clinical question, a description of the data (not the data itself), and a clinical contact who can answer questions about it. A 30-minute call is enough to tell whether the project is feasible.
