Dice score vs clinical error: interactive demo

The Dice score measures the pixel overlap between a predicted segmentation and the ground truth, from 0 to 1. Because it counts pixels, it is dominated by the largest structure, so a model can reach a high Dice while missing a small lesion completely. This demo puts a large and a small synthetic lesion in one image and reports Dice, boundary distances and the number of lesions detected as you change the prediction.

Where this matters

Segmentation and detection where small findings change the clinical decision: metastases, small nodules, early lesions. Report per-lesion detection and a boundary metric next to Dice, and choose metrics for the clinical question.

What this experiment models

Binary masks on a 160 × 160 grid with 1 mm pixels. The large true lesion is a slightly irregular disc; the prediction follows its boundary with a fixed wobble scaled by the roughness slider. Dice is 2|A∩B| / (|A| + |B|) over all pixels. Boundary distances are measured between the boundary pixels of the two masks in both directions; HD95 is the larger of the two directed 95th percentiles, and the maximum is the Hausdorff distance. A lesion counts as detected if any predicted pixel overlaps it.

The principle

How it works

The ground truth has one lesion about 68 mm across and one small lesion whose diameter you set. The prediction follows the large lesion with an adjustable boundary wobble, and either finds the small lesion or misses it. With an 8 mm lesion missed, Dice stays near 0.98, because the missing pixels are about 1% of the total.

Boundary metrics measure distance instead of overlap. For each boundary pixel of one mask, the demo finds the nearest boundary pixel of the other, in both directions. The Hausdorff distance is the largest of these distances, and HD95 is the larger of the two 95th percentiles, which ignores a few outlying pixels. A missed lesion far from the prediction produces large distances, but if its boundary is less than 5% of the total, HD95 ignores it too, while the maximum distance does not.

The clinical question is often simpler: was each lesion found? Counting detected lesions, and reporting per-lesion results next to Dice and a boundary metric, catches what a single overlap score hides. See medical imaging AI.
