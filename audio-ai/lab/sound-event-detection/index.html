Sound event detection: interactive demo

Sound event detection finds when target sounds occur in a stream of audio and marks where each one starts and ends. This demo runs a simple energy detector over an eight-second synthetic scene of knocks, beeps and low-frequency rumbles and scores it against ground truth, so you can see how the feature and the threshold trade hits against false alarms.

Where it’s used

Always-on detectors on phones, wearables and sensors, often as the cheap first stage before a model. See on-device audio AI.

What this experiment models

An 8-second, 16 kHz synthetic scene: pink background noise at the chosen RMS level (dBFS before playback volume), four damped multi-resonance knocks, four 2.6 kHz beeps and two band-limited low-frequency rumbles. Each 512-sample Hann-windowed frame (16 ms hop) gives a log energy, either over the whole band or over 1–6 kHz. The floor is the median over the clip. Frames above floor + threshold are merged (gaps up to 3 frames, minimum 2 frames). A target counts as detected if any detection overlaps it; a detection that overlaps no target is a false alarm. This is a classical energy detector, not a trained model; learned detectors replace the feature and threshold, but the hit/false-alarm tradeoff remains.

The principle

How it works

The detector splits the audio into short frames, computes a log energy for each, and flags frames that rise a set number of decibels above the clip’s median level. Neighbouring flagged frames are merged into events. Watching broadband energy, the rumbles look like events and trigger false alarms; restricting the feature to the 1–6 kHz band, where the knocks and beeps carry their energy, ignores them. A lower threshold catches quieter targets and lets more noise through; a higher one does the reverse.

This trade-off is the central design decision for always-on detectors on phones, wearables and sensors. A cheap energy or band-energy stage often runs first and wakes a trained classifier only when something happens, which keeps power low. Learned detectors replace the hand-picked feature and threshold, but the trade-off remains: it moves into the choice of operating point. See on-device audio ai.
